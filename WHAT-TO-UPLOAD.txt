WHAT IS NEW SINCE THE LAST ZIP

1. PAYROLL  —  /payroll
-----------------------
  Onboarding has always asked "do you run payroll?" and the calendar has
  always said provident fund is due on the 15th. Neither could say how much.
  Now it can.

  People, their pay split the way the law cares about, and a month that works
  itself out: provident fund on basic and dearness allowance, employee
  insurance on the gross, professional tax by state, and an estimate of tax on
  salary under either regime. It posts to the ledger properly, so salaries
  stop being a lump sum and an employer can finally see what staff cost —
  gross plus the contributions the business pays on top.

  Three bits of arithmetic people get wrong by hand, now tested:
    - the employer's 12% does not split 8.33/3.67 of the actual wage. The
      pension share is 8.33% of the ceiling wage however high the salary.
    - somebody who rises above the insurance ceiling partway through a
      half-year keeps contributing until it ends. This catches employers out
      at appraisal time.
    - the fund's administration charge has a floor per establishment, not per
      person, so a small payroll pays the minimum rather than half a per cent.

  Where a rule is not held — a professional tax slab for a state we have not
  encoded — the payslip says so instead of deducting nil. A wrong nil and a
  correct nil look identical on a payslip, which is how an employer finds out
  at assessment. The provident fund's own upload file is produced too.

2. STOCK AND COST OF GOODS  —  /stock
-------------------------------------
  A shop that buys to sell cannot read its profit from its bank account.
  Stock bought in March and sold in May is not March's cost — it is an asset
  until it sells. Without that every monthly profit figure is wrong in both
  directions.

  Switch an item on and purchases of it become stock rather than a cost of the
  month, and the cost appears in the month the thing actually left the shop,
  as one entry a month rather than one per sale. Sales are read from the
  invoices themselves, matched by code where there is one, because asking
  somebody to record the same sale twice is asking them to disagree with
  themselves.

  Cost is the weighted average: every delivery re-averages the shelf, every
  sale leaves at that average. It is one of the two methods the standards
  allow and the one a shopkeeper can check by hand.

  It also says when you have sold more than you bought, which means a purchase
  has not been recorded and the cost of those sales is missing from profit.
  And it warns when something is running low.

  A business that tracks nothing behaves exactly as it did before, so nothing
  can be counted twice.

3. E-INVOICING  —  /e-invoicing
-------------------------------
  Above a turnover threshold a B2B invoice is not valid until the government's
  portal has given it a number, and the customer cannot claim credit on one
  that was never registered.

  This works out whether the threshold applies from your own turnover, picks
  the invoices that actually belong on the portal (counter sales never do),
  builds the payload, and checks it before you send it — the number format,
  both GSTINs against their state codes, addresses, PIN codes, HSN codes, the
  rate against the portal's own list, and every total against its parts within
  the rupee of tolerance the portal allows. All at once, which matters because
  the portal reports one fault at a time.

  Nothing is registered from here. That needs credentials from the portal that
  NXOne does not have, and telling somebody an invoice was filed when it was
  not would be worse than not offering it.


ONE FAULT THIS FOUND IN ITS OWN CODE
  The e-invoice turnover thresholds were every one of them ten times too
  small — ₹500 crore written as ₹50 crore, and so on down. The test that
  printed the figure in crores caught it. They are right now.
