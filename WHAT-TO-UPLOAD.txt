1. CLOSING THE BOOKS ON A FILED PERIOD
--------------------------------------
  Nothing stopped a filed month from being edited, so the books and the return
  could drift apart silently and the first you would hear of it is a notice.

  Settings → Closing the books sets a date. Everything on or before it is
  closed: no edits, no deletions, no payments recorded into it, and no new
  document dated inside it — a document can be moved into a closed period as
  easily as edited inside one, and both are stopped. The GST returns screen
  offers to close the books when a return is filed, which is the moment it
  matters.

  Refusals say what to do instead — a credit note dated today, not an edit to
  what was filed — and name the dates the way you read them. Moving the lock
  back is allowed but never silent: it says what it is reopening.

2. INPUT CREDIT, CHECKED AGAINST GSTR-2B
----------------------------------------
  The only GST work that cannot come from your own records, because it is
  about whether your suppliers filed. Upload the portal's GSTR-2B JSON and it
  matches bill by bill: what agrees, what your supplier has not filed (credit
  at risk), what is on the portal that you never recorded, what the portal has
  blocked, and what cannot be matched at all. Each supplier who has not filed
  gets a written message naming the invoices and what they are worth.

  Matching ignores punctuation and padding, so INV/2026/0012 and inv-2026-12
  are one invoice. Two traps in the portal's format are handled: `val` is the
  whole invoice including tax, not the taxable value, and credit notes sit in
  a separate section and count the other way. An unrecorded credit note is
  never presented as credit to claim — it reduces what you may claim.

  Bills now carry the supplier's GSTIN, checked as you type.

3. TAX HELD BACK FROM SUPPLIERS
-------------------------------
  Mark the section on a bill and the amount to hold back is worked out, with
  the year's earlier payments to that supplier taken into account — the
  threshold is on the year, not on the bill in front of you. Under 194C
  nothing is due until payments pass ₹1,00,000, and then it is due on all of
  them, including those already paid in full. That retrospective step is what
  catches businesses out, so a supplier getting close is flagged before the
  next bill rather than after.

  The screen shows what should have been held back and was not, when each
  deposit is due (the 7th, except March), and a 26Q by deductee for any
  quarter. Tax held back is now posted as owed to the government rather than
  as cash paid out, so the balance sheet shows the liability.

  Rates and thresholds are read from published rate charts, not from the
  department's own notification, so every rule carries its date and source and
  the screen says plainly that they are unverified.

4. PAYMENT LINKS ON INVOICES
----------------------------
  A link covering cards, net banking and UPI, which works from abroad and —
  the point — tells us when the money arrived, so the invoice marks itself
  paid. One live link per invoice: a second link for the same bill is how a
  customer pays twice.

  An unsigned webhook records nothing. A payment that would take an invoice
  past its total, or that lands on a cancelled or already-settled invoice, is
  shown rather than applied: money appearing twice is harder to unpick than
  money appearing a day late.

  This needs the API deployed and a Razorpay account. Until then the button
  does not appear and the UPI code works as before.

  The three places that narrowed an expense for the ledger by hand are now one
