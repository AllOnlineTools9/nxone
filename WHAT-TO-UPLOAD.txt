6. HISTORY
----------
  Books that can be edited silently are books nobody can rely on. The question
  is never who did it — on one device it is always you — it is what this
  invoice said before somebody changed it, which comes up whenever a figure is
  queried months later.

  Recorded inside the store's own write methods rather than by their callers,
  so nothing can change through NXOne without leaving a trace: a trail
  somebody has to remember to write has holes exactly where it matters. Saves
  that changed nothing are not recorded. Changes that move the books are
  marked, and the last 500 are kept — older history is dropped rather than
  filling the space the books need.

  A bug caught here: several writes can land in the same millisecond, and
  sorting on the timestamp alone ordered those arbitrarily, so the newest was
  not reliably first. The stored order now breaks the tie.

7. CLAIMS
---------
  Money your people spent that you owe them back. The cost is dated the day
  the money was spent, not the day it was approved — a September lunch stays a
  September cost however long it waits — and until it is repaid it sits as
  owed to the person rather than paid out of a bank account.

  The figure shown is not the total claimed, it is how long somebody has been
  out of pocket. Claims cannot move backwards: once repaid, a correction is a
  correction rather than a rewrite of what happened.

8. TCS
------
  The mirror of TDS: added to a customer's bill, collected, and paid over. On
  ordinary goods it starts only once your turnover passes ₹10 crore and you
  have sold more than ₹50 lakh to one customer in a year; a scrap or timber
  dealer meets it from the first sale.

  Charged only on the part above the threshold. Charging the whole amount is
  the usual mistake, and it overcharges the customer rather than the
  government. On the /tds screen, under what you hold back.

9. CUSTOMER PORTAL
------------------
  A link a customer can open to see their invoice — no account, no password,
  nothing of the business behind it beyond that one document.

  Two decisions worth knowing. The link is a credential, so it is long,
  unguessable, avoids characters people misread aloud, is compared in constant
  time, and can be withdrawn. And what is shared is a snapshot: changing the
  invoice afterwards does not silently change what the customer was shown,
  which protects both sides of an argument about what was sent.

  The snapshot is built field by field on the device rather than by copying
  the document and deleting what should not go — under the second approach a
  field added later would find its own way into a stranger's browser. The
  server checks it again on arrival and refuses anything carrying a job id, a
  cost, a supplier or an internal note.

  Dormant until the API is deployed: the share button does not appear, and
  /view says there is nothing at the link.
